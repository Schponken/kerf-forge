## Decisions this supports

1. **Lap knuckles are a deliberate departure from every studied open finger — keep them only with axial retention.**
   Raptor (R1, ~0.4 mm/side) and Aero (R2, ~0.1 mm/side) both use fork/tongue knuckles, which are double shear and
   symmetric (R13, R14). The lap is justified here only by support-free printing on the side (Aero needs tree
   supports, R3; Brunel ships parts rotated ~45 deg, INDEX). Cost (calc): pin tilt in a 7.85 mm free bore with 0.35 mm
   clearance = atan(0.35/7.85) = **2.6 deg** vs ~1.3 deg if the free bore spanned 16 mm; face tilt over the 0.3 mm gap
   at disc radius 7.5 = **2.3 deg** -> ~1.8 mm sideways at a point 46 mm out. Mitigations that the sources support:
   full-disc bearing faces (already 15 mm), a small axial gap (0.3 mm total, between Aero 0.2 and Raptor 0.8 total),
   and **something that clamps the two laps together** (see Finding A).
2. **3 mm hardened dowel pins, pressed in the parent, free in the child.** Steel pins over printed pins (Yale R10 steel
   dowels, Aero dowels in bearings, ORCA metal pins R8; Raptor printed pins wear, INDEX). Press +0.05 / free +0.35 on
   diameter from catalog `fdm_fits.pin`; R15 says PETG wants +0.10-0.20 more diametral than PLA, so print a coupon
   with the free hole at 3.35 / 3.45 / 3.55 before the final print.
3. **Segment lengths 44 / 25 / 21 mm**: inside Aero 43 / 22 / 26.8 (INDEX), R7's model 42 / 27 / 19.5, and the adult
   bone means (INDEX). Width 16 mm vs Aero 16.4 / 15.2 / 14.0 measured.
4. **Tendon moment arms 4.0 mm flexor, 4.98 mm extensor are at the low end.** Raptor (child size) ~3.8 mm (R1); a
   printed research finger 4.3-10.5 mm (R6); Aero flexor 12.49 / 7.32 / 9.0 mm/rad (R3); human FDP 15.5 / 10.7 /
   7.7 mm (R4). Small arms mean low excursion and high tension. Excursion for a full curl (calc, at the home arms):
   flexor >= 4.0 x 4.01 rad = **16 mm**, extensor 4.98 x 4.01 = **20 mm** (Aero needs 44.45 mm with its larger arms).
5. **Tendon tension sizing (calc, this geometry, tip force perpendicular to the distal segment, flexor alone):**
   tension per newton of tip force = lever / 4.0 mm. Straight finger: MCP 90 mm -> **22.5 N/N**, PIP 11.5, DIP 5.2.
   30/30/30 pose: MCP 16.2. 60/60/60 pose: MCP 2.9, PIP 8.4, DIP 5.2. So a 12 N tip force on a straight finger needs
   ~270 N of flexor tension and a curled grasp needs ~100 N, which matches Aero's "~12 N <-> ~100 N" (R3). Expect
   only ~60 % to get through channel friction (R6). DESIGN.md section 7's "~80 N" covers only the DIP; the MCP governs.
6. **Line: braided UHMWPE/Spectra or Vectran, 0.5-0.8 mm, sized to the tension in decision 5 with SF >= 2.** Open
   designs: InMoov 200 lb braid (INDEX), Raptor 80 lb, Yale 100 lb Power Pro < 0.7 mm, ORCA 0.4 mm nylon (R8), Aero
   680 N Vectran (R3); 0.30 mm saws into the plastic (R9). PowerPro 80 lb is 0.43 mm (R11). A 30 kg (~295 N) line is
   SF ~1 for a straight-finger 12 N push, so specify **>= 100 lb (~445 N)** braid, or rate the finger at ~5 N straight
   and ~12 N curled. HMPE creeps (R12), so plan to re-tension (R8, R9).
7. **Channels 2.2 mm with a pointed roof** = ~3-4x a 0.5-0.6 mm line; Raptor's are 2.2 x 2.25 mm (R1). The
   swept-chord grooves that keep the line off plastic at every angle do the job ORCA and Yale do with steel routing
   pins (R8, R10).
8. **Termination: knot in a pocket + CA is proven** (InMoov, R9; the pocket fixes the reported knot shift). Yale and
   Brunel terminate on a screw (R10, INDEX) so the line can be re-tensioned; worth adding if the actuator has no
   tensioner.
9. **Material PETG.** R17: PETG HDT 65 C vs PLA 54 C, Vicat 78 vs 57 C, about twice the XY impact energy. R18: e-NABLE's
   kit supplier recommends PETG; PLA deforms in a hot car. R19: PLA creeps and keeps 1.5 mm of permanent set after a
   week at 29 C. PETG is less stiff (1790 vs 2750 MPa bending), so check the FEA deflection. Aero uses PLA and accepts
   that the phalanges yield first (R3).

## Findings that should change the design

- **A. Nothing holds the lap joints together along the pin (design-breaking).** In `fingerlib.py` the parent's disc is
  in band `s`, the child's in band `-s`, the dowel is pressed only into the parent and runs free through the child, and
  the child's other band-`s` material lies beyond x = 8.5 mm, past the end of the parent. So the child can slide off
  the free end of the pin; DESIGN.md step 2 ("slide the proximal's knuckle onto the protruding pin end") confirms it.
  Tendon tension has no component along the pin, so it will not hold the joint. A fork joint traps the tongue (R1,
  R2); a lap does not. Fixes that use existing hardware: (a) an M3 screw as the pivot, free in the child with the head
  counterbored on the child's outer face, threaded into a heat-set insert or nut in the parent (`iface.pin_hole`
  supports `fit="heatset"/"tap"`; InMoov accepts 3 mm screws, INDEX); a low head keeps the free-bore length near
  7.85 mm, since a 3 mm counterbore would raise the tilt to ~4 deg; (b) keep the dowel, insert it from the child's
  outer face and close that face with a glued printed cap. The pins check should also prove the joint is captured
  along the pin.
- **B. Flexor tension is underestimated (decision 5).** Either increase the flexor moment arm at the MCP (base and
  proximal can carry it further palmar, toward 7 mm, inside the 15 mm depth), specify >= 100 lb line, or rate the
  fingertip force by pose.
- **C. Flexor and extensor excursions differ (16 vs 20 mm, calc)**, and the flexor arm grows as the joint bends. One
  servo wheel driving both lines (InMoov style) will either go slack or bind. Use separate take-ups, a spring on the
  extensor, or match the arms.

## Open questions / numbers Spencer should measure

- Real free-hole size for the 3 mm dowel in PETG on the A2L: print a coupon at 3.35 / 3.45 / 3.55 mm (R15 suggests
  PETG runs looser than catalog +0.35). Same for the press hole (3.05 vs 3.00).
- Side rock of a printed lap pair under a ~5 N side push at the tip (predicted ~2-3 deg per joint). If it is too
  much, go to a 3-layer fork printed flat with a teardrop pin hole (R15) or rotated ~45 deg (Brunel).
- Diameter and break strength of the line actually bought (100-200 lb PowerPro diameters UNVERIFIED, R11).
- Actuator: available pull force and stroke vs ~100-270 N and 16-20 mm (decisions 4-5).
- Long-term PETG creep under constant tendon load: no PETG creep test found (R19 tested PLA, ABS, ASA and CF blends
  only). Re-check tendon slack after a week.
- No vendor STEP was fetched for the ISO 8734 dowel; the catalog proxy (d 3.0, stocked 16 mm) is enough for checks.
