# Reference library

Every open design Kerf Forge has studied, so later projects reuse the research instead of repeating it. The
design-researcher reads this first and appends every new design it studies (one row each, all columns filled);
`tests/test_references.py` enforces that the table is complete and that every design linked from a project's
REFERENCES.md is listed here.

Columns: **Key dimensions** say how each number was obtained — *measured* (bounding box / section of the published
STL/STEP/DXF/3MF with trimesh or build123d, repo shallow-cloned) or *read* (stated on the linked page or file).
**License** is as stated by the source; "not stated" means reference only — borrow ideas and published numbers, never
copy geometry. Research date for the first batch: 2026-10-30.

## Cross-cutting lessons

Robot arms and pan-tilts
- Shoulder torque binds first: 150 g × 25 cm = 3.75 kg·cm payload alone; with ~55 g servos and links the total nears
  MG996R stall (9.4 kg·cm @4.8 V). SO-101's 16.5 kg·cm servos give only ~200 g real payload. Use a higher-torque servo
  and/or a counterbalance, or move servo mass to the base (EEZYbot four-bar). Sources: SO-ARM100 #56, HowToMechatronics.
- Never let a servo spline carry radial or overturning load: bearings under the yaw (Moveo 608 ring, Thor, EEZYbot
  balls) and at pan/tilt axes (inertiarobotics A/B).
- Expect ~1° backlash and pose-dependent sag; put the stiffest, shortest load path at the shoulder (SO-ARM100 #134, Thor FAQ).
- Print fit is the commonest assembly failure: model horn pockets from catalog numbers with clearance, print fit gauges
  first (SO-101), screw horns rather than glue.
- Power: ≥2 A per pair of hobby servos, a bulk capacitor stops brownouts (HowToMechatronics, Pimoroni, traxrobotics).
- 1/4-20 tripod mounts: no printed design uses a printed thread; use a captive brass nut/insert (Adafruit Pi camera
  tripod case uses a purchased adapter screw).

Multirotors and TVC
- Printed arms need far more section than carbon (carbon 5.5-6 mm thick, tapering ~16 → 12.4 mm wide); size printed
  arms by stiffness, not just strength; expect a printed 5" frame at ~150-190 g. Arms fail at the root: generous
  root fillets, taper wider toward the root, bolt-on swappable arms clamped between plates, solid motor pads.
- Materials from crash reports: PLA/PETG crack, unfilled nylon twists, PETG-CF fine, PA-CF/PAHT-CF best; heat-set
  inserts at motor screws; metal standoffs; soft-mount the stack (grommets).
- Model prop discs from the real catalog prop; 5" frames at 220-240 mm diagonal leave ~25-30 mm between 5.1" tips.
- TVC: working coax drones clamp the gimbal at ~±8.5° (fd.iwth, Mistral), rocket gimbals ±5°, vanes ±15°. MG90S
  metal-gear servos are the proven choice; nested gimbal outer gear changes the effective ratio. Keep the pivot well
  below the CG and mass at the ends (battery high, motors low). Coax T/W in flown builds ~1.3-2. A coax pair is ~22% less efficient than two isolated rotors; prop-plane spacing
  beyond 0.25 D gains nothing (MDPI Aerospace 3(4):32).
- Carbon-rod masts: 3 mm rods flex at ~0.5 m (use 5 mm); rod fit needs ~0.5 mm total clearance plus a mechanical lock
  (screw into a notch, bolt), not a press fit (Starkit, fd.iwth). Printed pivot pins beat metal screws for gimbal
  friction (arXiv 2509.00061).

Tendon fingers
- No line over sharp plastic: every channel bend a smooth radius ≥ 2.5 mm or a steel pin (InMoov, Yale, Aero).
- Channel ~2-3× the line diameter (1.5-2 mm for 0.5-0.8 mm braid), drillable/reamable along its axis (inference from
  Yale 1.6 mm ports for <0.7 mm line, Raptor slots).
- Steel pins beat printed/filament pins for wear and alignment; press/glue in the outer knuckles, ~0.2-0.5 mm
  diametral running clearance in the moving part (InMoov 3.0/3.5, Yale reamed fits, Aero dowels in bearings).
- Return: active antagonist (re-tension as line creeps), elastic cord (tune per user), or springs (consistent);
  softer MCP spring closes proximal-first (Aero).
- Moment arm: flexor ~4-8 mm palm-side of each pin, extensor ~3-5 mm dorsal (inference); Aero needs 44.45 mm travel
  for 3 × 90° (~9.4 mm average arm). Check the actuator travel.
- Support-free knuckles: print on the side (pin axis vertical) for round bores, but layers then cross the clevis ears —
  check their bending (Brunel ships parts rotated ~45°).

Grippers (parallel jaw, rack and pinion)
- One pinion between two racks on opposite sides of it, one rack per jaw carriage, both carriages on the same two
  rods (Robo9) or in printed channels (Printables 403482, GripSense): the jaws stay parallel and self-centre.
- Never press a printed pinion onto a bare servo spline: it spins (403482 user reports). Screw it to a metal horn or
  a servo disc (Robo9: 4 set screws through the disc into the gear).
- Back the racks so they cannot spring away from the pinion under load (Robo9: bearings behind the racks, per renders).
- Size rod bores from a printed test plate and the real rod (Robo9 6.0-6.3 test plate), not from the nominal diameter.
- Limit force in software, not by stalling: torque limit (Robo9 Max Torque Limit 500, GripSense 120 units), stop at contact
  (AR Gripper), limit the closing angle (SG90 claw). Optionally a deliberate weak link (GripSense fingers snap > 3 kg).
- Full stroke on hobby servos needs most of the servo's travel (Pololu 0.5-2.4 ms; SG90 claw 180 deg): pick the
  pinion so the stroke fits the standard range with margin.
- Grip pads: 2-3.3 mm compliant layer (flex filament pads, foam rubber + grip tape, ROBOTIS rubber pads). Bare PETG
  on steel has mu ~0.24-0.29 (Stoimenov 2024); rubber on cardboard 0.5-0.8 (Engineering ToolBox).

Geared claws (one servo, two fingers coupled 1:1)
- The servo turns one finger; a 1:1 gear pair turns the other. Squeeze per finger = servo torque / (2 × contact
  radius), half of what the same servo gives one finger against a fixed jaw (virtual work). MG996R at 50 % of stall:
  4.2 N at 55 mm, 5.1 N at 45 mm.
- Precedents hold both finger axes at both ends: MeArm V3 (servo plate + bottom plate), Vector Concept (top + bottom
  plates, Ø6 bores), Printables 912645 (coaxial bores 54 mm apart). EEZYbot MK2 (one plate) is the exception.
- Printed claw gears in use: module 1.5 with 10-16 teeth (EEZYbot, 912645, 5-7 mm faces), module 2 × 12 teeth × 15 mm
  (1645415). Screw the drive finger to a metal horn; never press a printed gear onto the bare spline.
- Friction sets the payload: printed PLA/PETG jaws μ 0.15-0.29 on steel; TPU 30D 0.46-0.79 (knurled best), TPE 88
  0.25-0.51 (Suder 2021, Stoimenov 2024). Harder TPU 95A will be lower: tilt-test it.
- Pads: slide-in TPU dovetail with ~0.15-0.2 mm per side (Clamp-On Vise, Suder inserts), glued ribbed strips (912645),
  or rubber on tape (127059).
- Pad faces must stay within ±tan⁻¹ μ of parallel over the grasp range or converge toward the tips (Modern Robotics
  12.2.1, derived). Never hold an object at stall: the servo heats within minutes (Pololu forum).
